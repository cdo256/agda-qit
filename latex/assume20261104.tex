\section*{Aim and pacing}

The talk should present the architecture of the reduction rather than every
constructor calculation.  Plan for roughly 50 minutes of material and leave
the remaining 10 minutes for questions.  The audience should leave with three
ideas:
\[
\begin{gathered}
  \PropLift\text{ implements }G,
  \qquad
  \Obs\text{ gives the correct morphisms},\\
  \beta\text{ plus initiality proves the round trip}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Use context extension as the single representative constructor.  State that
the universe, substitution, product, and substitution-type cases have the
same proof shape.

\section*{Board 1: The problem (0--10 minutes)}

Write the two presentations schematically:
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
  \text{Mutual} &:& \Con,\ \Ty,\ \mathsf{ty}_1,\ \bullet,\
                    \mathbin{\triangleright},\ \mathsf{u},\ \pi,\ \sigma,\\[2mm]
  \text{Weakly tagged} &:& \CT,\ [-],\ \widehat c,\ \widehat t,\
                    \mathsf{ty}_1,\ \bullet,\ \mathbin{\triangleright},\
                    \mathsf{u},\ \pi,\ \sigma.
\end{array}
\]
State the theorem immediately:
\[
  I\text{ initial in }\Weak
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  F(I)\text{ initial in }\Mutual.
\]
Draw the functors and leave this diagram visible throughout:
\[
  \Weak \mathrel{\mathop{\rightleftarrows}^{F}_{G}} \Mutual.
\]
Explain the strict-positivity motivation verbally.  Write only the formation
rule for extension:
\[
  \frac{[\gamma]=\widehat c\qquad [a]=\widehat t\qquad
        \mathsf{ty}_1(a)=\gamma}
       {[\gamma\mathbin{\triangleright}a]=\widehat c}.
\]
Do not write the analogous rules for the other constructors.

\section*{Board 2: The functors and the easy round trip (10--20 minutes)}

For the projection functor write:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  F(A).\Con &\;=\; \sum_{\gamma:A.\CT}[\gamma]=\widehat c,\\
  F(A).\Ty  &\;=\; \sum_{a:A.\CT}[a]=\widehat t.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the reverse construction write only:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mathsf{Atom}(A)
    &\;=\; \mathsf{con}(A.\Con)+\mathsf{ty}(A.\Ty)
          +\{\widehat k,\widehat c,\widehat t\},\\
  G(A).\CT &\;=\; \PropLift(\mathsf{Atom}(A)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Describe \(\PropLift\) as a partial element whose definedness is a
proposition.  State, without proving its internal witness calculations, the
natural isomorphism
\[
  \varepsilon_A:F(G(A))\cong A.
\]
Its context and type components simply extract the unique context or type atom.

\section*{Board 3: Why ordinary morphisms fail (20--30 minutes)}

This is the central motivation.  Write:
\[
  \mathsf{ty}_1(a)\neq\gamma,
  \qquad
  f(\mathsf{ty}_1(a))=f(\gamma).
\]
A non-injective semantic map can therefore make
\[
  \gamma\mathbin{\triangleright}a\text{ undefined},
  \qquad
  f(\gamma)\mathbin{\triangleright}f(a)\text{ defined}.
\]
Consequently, unconditional preservation of raw operations is false.  On the
other hand, preservation conditional on the expected input witnesses is too
weak: from a hypothetical wrong output tag
\[
  [\gamma\mathbin{\triangleright}a]=\widehat t
\]
one cannot obtain the expected input witnesses needed to invoke the
homomorphism law.

\section*{Board 4: Observable morphisms (30--38 minutes)}

Write prominently:
\[
  \Obs_A(x)
  \;:\!\!\equiv\;
  ([x]=\widehat c)\;\lor\;([x]=\widehat t).
\]
Then write the extension law:
\[
  \Obs_A(\gamma\mathbin{\triangleright}a)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \theta(\gamma\mathbin{\triangleright}a)
  =\theta(\gamma)\mathbin{\triangleright}\theta(a).
\]
The slogan is:
\begin{quote}
  Preserve an operation exactly when its result is visible to the projected
  context/type theory.
\end{quote}
Explain verbally that malformed junk is ignored, non-injective maps remain
allowed, composition transports observability, and homomorphisms are compared
only on context- and type-classified points.

\section*{Board 5: The hard round trip (38--47 minutes)}

Let
\[
  \iota:I\longrightarrow G(F(I))
\]
be the unique weakly tagged morphism.  Write the two computational statements:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \beta_c(x)&:\ [x]=\widehat c
      \longrightarrow \iota(x)=\mathsf{return}(\mathsf{con}(x)),\\
  \beta_t(x)&:\ [x]=\widehat t
      \longrightarrow \iota(x)=\mathsf{return}(\mathsf{ty}(x)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Mention that these form a proposition-valued displayed algebra, so the path
constructor coherence is automatic.

Show only the wrong-sort extension case.  Given
\[
  p:[\gamma\mathbin{\triangleright}a]=\widehat t,
\]
we have \(\Obs_I(\gamma\mathbin{\triangleright}a)\), hence the operation law for
\(\iota\) applies.  Transporting \(p\) says that the corresponding concrete
extension in \(G(F(I))\) is type-tagged.  But direct analysis of the
\(\PropLift\) operation says every observable extension is context-tagged.
Thus \(\widehat c=\widehat t\), a contradiction.  State that the
\(\mathsf{u}\), \(\pi\), and \(\sigma\) cases are identical in shape.

Conclude:
\[
  F(\iota)=\varepsilon^{-1}_{F(I)},
\]
and therefore obtain both triangle identities.

\section*{Board 6: Transfer initiality (47--53 minutes)}

Define the chosen recursor:
\[
  \mathsf{rec}^{\Mutual}_A
  \;:\!\!\equiv\;
  \varepsilon_A\circ F(\mathsf{rec}^{\Weak}_{G(A)}).
\]
For an arbitrary \(f:F(I)\to A\), finish with the calculation
\[
\begin{aligned}
  f
  &\simeq (f\circ\varepsilon_{F(I)})\circ F(\iota)
      && \text{triangle identity}\\
  &\simeq (\varepsilon_A\circ F(Gf))\circ F(\iota)
      && \text{naturality of }\varepsilon\\
  &\simeq \varepsilon_A\circ F(Gf\circ\iota)
      && \text{functoriality of }F\\
  &\simeq \varepsilon_A\circ F(\mathsf{rec}^{\Weak}_{G(A)})
      && \text{initiality of }I\\
  &=\mathsf{rec}^{\Mutual}_A.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the payoff; stop the technical development here.

\section*{Final three minutes}

End with three statements:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A strictly positive weak syntax can recover the intended mutual syntax.
  \item Malformed raw terms force an observational notion of morphism.
  \item Output observability is the precise boundary between semantic structure
        and irrelevant junk.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Material to omit from the board}

Do not present the following unless asked:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the full definitions of \(\pi\) and \(\sigma\);
  \item witness-level calculations inside \(\PropLift\);
  \item universe lifting;
  \item proof-irrelevance and setoid-category boilerplate;
  \item every field of the displayed algebra;
  \item failed classifier constructions;
  \item the possible adjunction \(G\dashv F\);
  \item Agda terms longer than a single line.
\end{itemize}

Reserve one corner of the board for the theorem and the diagram
\(\Weak\rightleftarrows\Mutual\), and do not erase it.  If possible, provide a
one-page handout or repository link containing the complete signatures and
formal proof.
