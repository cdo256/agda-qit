\section*{Aim and pacing}

Plan for 50 minutes of material and leave 10 minutes for questions. Present
the architecture of the reduction rather than every constructor calculation.
The background replaces the opening of the previous plan; it is not an
additional introduction. Reach the main construction by minute 17.

The narrative is:
\begin{quote}
Why construct the theory of signatures? Why reduce it to CQW types?
How does the encoding work? Why does initiality survive?
\end{quote}
Use context extension as the single running constructor, from dependent
syntax through flattening and tagging to the initiality proof. Mention the
universe, substitution, and product cases without writing their complete
definitions. The audience should leave with three ideas:
\[
\begin{gathered}
  \PropLift\text{ implements }G,
  \qquad \Obs\text{ gives the required morphisms},\\
  \beta\text{ plus initiality proves the round trip}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Keep the distinctions between the reduction theorem, the existence of its
source initial algebra, and the generalisation to further signatures explicit.

\section*{Board 1: Motivation and roadmap (0--3 minutes)}

Introduce the project as work in progress on constructing quotient
inductive-inductive types. State the question:
\begin{quote}
Which assumptions suffice to construct QIITs, rather than taking them as
primitive type formers?
\end{quote}
Give the two relevant features verbally: sorts may depend on other sorts,
and constructors may impose equations. Infinitary branching is also in scope.
Mention term models of type theories as the principal application. Mobiles,
ordinals, and multisets can be mentioned as further motivation, but do not
develop a separate example here.

Write the overall route, with arrows read as construction dependencies:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \text{QIITs described by signatures}\\[-1mm]
 \Longleftarrow\ \text{initial syntax for a suitable }\ToS\\[-1mm]
 \Longleftarrow\ \text{initial weakly tagged CQW presentation}\\[-1mm]
 \Longleftarrow\ \text{inductive types, quotients, and WISC}\quad(*) .
\end{gathered}
\]
Point to the middle arrow as the focus of the talk. Label the final arrow
\((*)\): the published QW/QWI existence result motivates it, while its
extension to the required CQW presentation remains to be verified.

State the main theorem informally immediately:
\begin{quote}
An initial model of the weak presentation recovers an initial model of the
intended mutual presentation.
\end{quote}
Do not claim that this already establishes every arrow in the roadmap.

\section*{Board 2: Why the theory of signatures? (3--7 minutes)}

Explain the strategy of Kaposi, Kov\'acs, and Altenkirch
\cite{KKA19,KK20}: signatures can themselves be expressed in a small
domain-specific dependent type theory. Its syntax then supports the
construction of the QIITs it describes. Thus a suitable theory of signatures
provides a common construction problem rather than a separate construction
for every example.

Distinguish the settings briefly. The 2019 result treats finitary QIITs; the
2020 work treats large and infinitary signatures in an extensional
metatheory. Our intended presentation uses external function constructors
to handle infinitary branching in an intensional setting. State the working
metatheory once, following the background notes: intensional MLTT with
universes, judgemental UIP, a proposition universe, unique choice
(\(\mathsf{A!C}\)), function extensionality, and propositional
extensionality. Distinguish judgemental UIP from equality reflection.
External function types do not by themselves prove the universal
construction theorem for this variant.

Write only the part of the syntax needed for the running example:
\[
 \Con:\mathcal U,\qquad
 \Ty:\Con\to\mathcal U,\qquad
 \bullet:\Con,\qquad
 ({\ext}):(\Gamma:\Con)\to\Ty(\Gamma)\to\Con.
\]
Say that the full presentation also has terms, substitutions, a universe,
function constructors, identity structure, and equations. The point here is
the dependence of \(\Ty\) on \(\Con\), not the full syntax of a CwF.

\textbf{Transition.} We want an initial model of this dependent syntax.
The target construction machinery is designed for a simpler kind of algebra.

\section*{Board 3: The QW and CQW targets (7--10 minutes)}

Recall that a container \((S,P)\), with \(S:\mathcal U\) and
\(P:S\to\mathcal U\), specifies operations
\[
 \alpha:(s:S)\to(P(s)\to X)\to X.
\]
A QW signature adds equations between terms in the free algebra; a QW type
is an initial algebra satisfying those equations. A CQW signature allows
equations conditional on a family of other equations. Display only the
schematic form:
\[
 \bigl(\forall b:B,\ \ell_b[\rho]=r_b[\rho]\bigr)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ell[\rho]=r[\rho].
\]
Here \(\rho\) assigns values to variables. The premise family can be
infinitary. This is the form needed for typing and compatibility conditions.

Explain the construction motivation in two sentences. Fiore, Pitts, and
Steenkamp construct QW/QWI types from inductive types and quotients under
WISC, using well-founded approximations and their colimit \cite{FPS22}.
The present reduction is intended to bring dependent syntax into reach of
that machinery, after extending the existence argument to the required
conditional equations.

\textbf{Status to say aloud.} The published QW/QWI theorem is established;
the required CQW extension is a separate step still awaiting verification.
Do not present WISC's definition, the size construction, stage relations,
or the cocontinuity proof.

\section*{Board 4: From dependent syntax to weak syntax (10--17 minutes)}

This board supplies the bridge into the main construction. Use only context
extension, in the following three stages.

\subsection*{1. Flatten the dependent family}

Replace the family of types by its total space:
\[
 \Ty_{\mathrm{tot}}:=\sum_{\Gamma:\Con}\Ty(\Gamma),
 \qquad \tyone:\Ty_{\mathrm{tot}}\to\Con.
\]
The extension operation can then be written as
\[
 ({\ext}):(\Gamma:\Con)\to(a:\Ty_{\mathrm{tot}})
          \to(\tyone(a)=\Gamma)\to\Con.
\]
The indices have become projections and compatibility equations.
Explain the Cartmell-style total-space translation verbally
\cite{Cartmell86}; do not prove its equivalence of model categories here.
For infinitary signatures, identify the corresponding extension as part of
the argument being used, rather than attributing it without qualification
to the finitary result.

Let \(\Dependent\) denote the intended dependent model category and
\(\Mutual\) its flattened mutual presentation. From this point, abbreviate
the independent sort \(\Ty_{\mathrm{tot}}\) in \(\Mutual\) by \(\Ty\).
The original family is recovered by fibres:
\[
 \Ty_{\mathrm{dep}}(\Gamma)
   :=\sum_{a:\Ty}(\tyone(a)=\Gamma).
\]
Thus \(\Mutual\) is an intermediate target, not an unexplained replacement
for the original dependent syntax.

\subsection*{2. Combine the sorts using tags}

Introduce a single carrier and classifier:
\[
 \CT:\mathcal U,\qquad [-]:\CT\to\CT,\qquad
 \ctag,\ttag:\CT.
\]
Contexts and types will be the classifier fibres over \(\ctag\) and
\(\ttag\). At this stage the expected tags and the projection compatibility
can still appear as proof arguments to constructors. This removes multiple
carriers, but does not yet give the desired ordinary operation arities.

\subsection*{3. Move premises out of operation arguments}

Make the raw operations total:
\[
 \tyone:\CT\to\CT,\qquad
 ({\ext}):\CT\to\CT\to\CT,
\]
and place the typing information in conditional equations, for example
\[
 \frac{[\gamma]=\ctag\qquad [a]=\ttag\qquad
        \tyone(a)=\gamma}
      {[\gamma\ext a]=\ctag}.
\]
This is now the shape of a CQW specification: the raw operation has an
ordinary arity, and its formation law has equational premises.
State that the full presentation also includes the corresponding projection
and equation laws; do not enumerate them on the board.

\textbf{The consequence to emphasise.} Raw extension now exists even when
its inputs are malformed. The conditional law controls well-formed inputs;
it does not eliminate all junk from arbitrary models. Recovering the right
initial algebra therefore requires more than writing down the signature.

Write the two presentations schematically:
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
 \text{Mutual} &:& \Con,\Ty,\tyone,\bullet,{\ext},\mathsf u,\pi,\sigma,\\
 \text{Weakly tagged} &:& \CT,[-],\ctag,\ttag,
                         \tyone,\bullet,{\ext},\mathsf u,\pi,\sigma.
\end{array}
\]
Reserve a corner of the board for
\[
 \Weak\mathrel{\mathop{\rightleftarrows}^{F}_{G}}\Mutual,
 \qquad
 I\text{ initial in }\Weak
 \Longrightarrow F(I)\text{ initial in }\Mutual.
\]
Here \(\Weak\) will carry the observational morphisms explained below.
Leave the theorem and this diagram visible for the rest of the talk.

\section*{Board 5: Projection and reconstruction (17--24 minutes)}

For the projection functor write
\[
\begin{aligned}
 F(A).\Con&:=\sum_{\gamma:A.\CT}[\gamma]=\ctag,\\
 F(A).\Ty&:=\sum_{a:A.\CT}[a]=\ttag.
\end{aligned}
\]
The typing and projection laws ensure that the operations restrict to these
fibres, including the projection from types to contexts. Explain extension
as the representative case. Do not write all the functor fields.

For the reverse construction, start with atoms for the valid data:
\[
 \Atom(A):=A.\Con+A.\Ty+\{\kterm,\ctag,\ttag\}.
\]
Name the first two injections \(\mathsf{con}\) and \(\mathsf{ty}\).
Briefly explain why simply using \(\mathsf{Maybe}(\Atom(A))\) is insufficient:
constructing extension requires a compatibility equation, and an arbitrary
model need not have decidable equality. We must record conditional
definedness without deciding whether the condition holds.

Write
\[
 \PropLift(X):=\sum_{P:\mathsf{Prop}}(P\to X),
 \qquad G(A).\CT:=\PropLift(\Atom(A)).
\]
Describe this as a partial element whose definedness is a proposition.
Under the stated extensionality assumptions, its operations can require
tag and compatibility witnesses and return the corresponding semantic atom
when those witnesses are available. Suppress universe lifting and internal
witness calculations.

State the easy round trip as a natural isomorphism:
\[
 \varepsilon_A:F(G(A))\cong A.
\]
Its context and type components extract the unique corresponding atom.
Do not claim \(GF(A)\cong A\) for arbitrary weak models: projection forgets
junk, and reconstruction supplies its own partial raw elements.

\section*{Board 6: Morphisms and output observability (24--33 minutes)}

\subsection*{Why unconditional preservation fails}

A semantic map need not be injective. In a possible instance, write
\[
 \tyone(a)\ne\gamma,
 \qquad f(\tyone(a))=f(\gamma).
\]
The corresponding partial extension in \(G(A)\) can be undefined while
extension of the images in \(G(B)\) is defined. Hence \(G(f)\) cannot in
general preserve every raw operation unconditionally.

Preservation only under the expected input witnesses is also insufficient
for the intended proof. Given a hypothetical wrong output tag
\[
 [\gamma\ext a]=\ttag,
\]
we cannot obtain the expected input witnesses merely from this hypothesis,
so such a homomorphism law would not apply.

\subsection*{Preserve operations when their outputs are observable}

Write prominently
\[
 \Obs_A(x):\!\!\equiv([x]=\ctag)\lor([x]=\ttag),
\]
and the extension law
\[
 \Obs_A(\gamma\ext a)
 \Longrightarrow
 \theta(\gamma\ext a)=\theta(\gamma)\ext\theta(a).
\]
The slogan is:
\begin{quote}
Preserve an operation exactly when its result is visible to the projected
context/type theory.
\end{quote}
Explain verbally that the complete morphism definition also carries the
classifier compatibility needed to transport observability. Composition
transports observability; non-injective semantic maps remain allowed.
Morphisms are compared on context- and type-classified elements, rather than
on irrelevant raw junk. Do not write the setoid-category bookkeeping.

\textbf{Interface with CQW existence.} A CQW signature with ordinary
homomorphisms and the category \(\Weak\) with observational morphisms are
not automatically the same category. State which initiality result is being
assumed: the theorem below requires initiality in \(\Weak\). To obtain a
complete CQW-to-QIIT existence theorem, one must justify how the constructed
CQW syntax supplies that initiality, including uniqueness for the chosen
morphisms. Keep this interface separate from the pending extension of the
WISC existence construction. Do not silently infer either step from the
published QW result.

\section*{Board 7: The hard round trip (33--42 minutes)}

Let \(I\) be initial in \(\Weak\), and let
\[
 \iota:I\longrightarrow G(F(I))
\]
be its unique morphism to the reconstructed model. State the two
computational properties, keeping the fibre witnesses explicit:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \beta_c(x,p):\quad
   \iota(x)&=\ret\bigl(\mathsf{con}(x,p)\bigr)
       &&(p:[x]=\ctag),\\
 \beta_t(x,p):\quad
   \iota(x)&=\ret\bigl(\mathsf{ty}(x,p)\bigr)
       &&(p:[x]=\ttag).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \((x,p)\) is an element of the relevant sort of \(F(I)\).
Explain that these are proved using a proposition-valued displayed algebra.
Use the proposition-valued induction principle for the initial syntax;
if the starting assumption is categorical initiality alone, state the
initiality-to-induction lemma in the chosen algebra semantics. With the
stated UIP assumptions, the required path coherence is automatic once the
point-constructor cases have been supplied.

For extension, briefly describe the expected-sort case: the induction
hypotheses return the context and type atoms, and compatibility permits
the reconstructed extension to return the required context atom.

Then show only the wrong-sort case in detail. Suppose
\[
 p:[\gamma\ext a]=\ttag.
\]
This gives \(\Obs_I(\gamma\ext a)\), so the operation law for \(\iota\)
applies without first obtaining the expected input witnesses. Classifier
compatibility and that operation law make the corresponding concrete
extension in \(G(F(I))\) type-tagged. But the concrete partial operation
can have an observable result only by returning a context atom. A context
atom cannot be type-tagged, giving a contradiction between the distinct
concrete tags. Emphasise that this is the purpose of the output condition.

State that the other constructor cases follow the same proof pattern;
omit their calculations. Conclude
\[
 F(\iota)=\varepsilon^{-1}_{F(I)},
\]
or equivalently the two inverse identities
\[
 \varepsilon_{F(I)}\circ F(\iota)=1_{F(I)},
 \qquad F(\iota)\circ\varepsilon_{F(I)}=1_{FGF(I)}.
\]
These concern this initial object. They do not assert a global equivalence
between all weak and mutual models.

\section*{Board 8: Transfer initiality (42--48 minutes)}

For a mutual model \(A\), define the chosen recursor
\[
 \mathsf{rec}^{\Mutual}_A
 :=\varepsilon_A\circ F\bigl(\mathsf{rec}^{\Weak}_{G(A)}\bigr).
\]
Existence is now immediate: interpret the weak initial syntax in \(G(A)\),
project, and use the easy round trip.

For uniqueness, take an arbitrary \(f:F(I)\to A\) and calculate
\[
\begin{aligned}
 f
 &\simeq(f\circ\varepsilon_{F(I)})\circ F(\iota)
       &&\text{inverse identity}\\
 &\simeq(\varepsilon_A\circ F(Gf))\circ F(\iota)
       &&\text{naturality of }\varepsilon\\
 &\simeq\varepsilon_A\circ F(Gf\circ\iota)
       &&\text{functoriality of }F\\
 &\simeq\varepsilon_A\circ F\bigl(\mathsf{rec}^{\Weak}_{G(A)}\bigr)
       &&\text{initiality of }I\\
 &=\mathsf{rec}^{\Mutual}_A.
\end{aligned}
\]
Say what makes the final use of initiality possible: both
\(Gf\circ\iota\) and \(\mathsf{rec}^{\Weak}_{G(A)}\) are morphisms
from \(I\) to \(G(A)\). Thus \(F(I)\) is initial in \(\Mutual\).
The total-space/fibre correspondence then returns the intended dependent
presentation, provided that correspondence has been established for the
signature under discussion. Stop the technical development here.

\section*{Board 9: Conclusion and status (48--50 minutes)}

Return to the roadmap from Board 1. End with three statements:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Flattening and weak tagging turn dependent syntax into ordinary raw
       operations governed by conditional equations.
 \item Malformed raw terms require observational morphisms; output
       observability supplies exactly the operation law used in the proof.
 \item Propositional partial elements construct \(G\); the \(\beta\) argument
       and initiality establish the required round trip and transfer
       initiality.
\end{enumerate}
State the scope honestly: the displayed transfer theorem is the main
construction being explained. The complete existence route additionally
requires the CQW extension of the WISC construction, the interface to
observational initiality, and the relevant generality of the flattening
and theory-of-signatures steps. Report the current proof/formalisation
status of those steps without presenting the entire route as finished.
